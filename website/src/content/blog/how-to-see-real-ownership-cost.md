Car costs are easy to underestimate. Fill-ups happen often and are obvious at once. Insurance, seasonal work, and small purchases are spread across the year. To see the whole picture, they need to be counted together.

## Group costs by what they are for

A few simple categories are enough:

- fuel;
- planned maintenance;
- unplanned repairs;
- tyres and seasonal work;
- insurance, taxes, and parking;
- accessories and washing.

A system that is too detailed gets tiring. Keep only as many categories as help you decide.

## Always record the mileage

The date shows when the money was spent. The mileage connects that spend to how the car is used. Together they show what the car cost per kilometre and how often service repeats.

Mileage matters most for oil, filters, brakes, tyres, and suspension parts.

## Look at a long period

One quiet month says little about the cost of ownership. Six months is more useful, and a full year is better. Seasonal costs and large planned jobs then fall inside the period.

An unusually expensive month is not always a problem. You may have paid the insurance or replaced a set of tyres that will last several seasons.

## Separate facts from forecasts

Recorded costs show the past. For planning, keep a separate list of upcoming work and a rough price. That reserve lets you prepare for a large service without an unpleasant surprise.

My Auto keeps fill-ups and repairs next to the car’s history, so the actual figures are always there to check.
