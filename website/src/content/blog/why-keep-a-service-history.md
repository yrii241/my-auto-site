A car rarely fails “out of nowhere.” Problems usually build up: a fluid change that was forgotten, a noise that was never checked, a part with no clear service life. A service history turns scattered events into a picture you can read.

## Service becomes predictable

Recording the date, the mileage, and the work answers the main question: **what was done to the car, and when**. You do not have to guess whether the brake fluid was changed one year ago or three.

The minimum for each entry:

- the date and the current mileage;
- the name of the work;
- the parts and materials used;
- the cost;
- a photo of the receipt or the part, when you need one.

That is enough to judge service intervals and plan the next visit to the shop.

## Costs are easier to follow

One large bill from the shop is easy to remember. Small regular costs are not. When every entry is in one place, the real cost of keeping the car becomes visible.

That record helps you compare periods, notice a fault that keeps coming back, and decide without guessing. For example, whether to keep repairing a component or replace it.

## Fewer arguments with the shop

A history with dates, mileage, and photos lets you explain exactly what happened to the car before. That matters when a problem returns, or when the car was serviced in more than one place.

Instead of “I think this was already replaced,” you have a specific entry.

## More trust when you sell

A buyer cares about more than the paint and the odometer. A continuous record of the work shows that the car was looked after. Entries with photos and documents make that account easier to believe.

The history in My Auto can be saved as a PDF and shown without handing over a personal phone.

## Start from where the car is now

You do not need to rebuild the car’s whole life in one evening. Add the current mileage, the latest work you know about, and the next planned service. The new history starts today.
